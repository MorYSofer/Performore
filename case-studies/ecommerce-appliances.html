Case study / Ecommerce · Premium home appliances

More than double the units sold. Year over year.

A premium home-appliance retailer selling to homes and to workplaces. We rebuilt the acquisition system around it — the offer, the data, the job of each channel and the way creative learned.

Results · year over year
+117% Units sold, year over year 2.2× the units of the same period a year earlier.
+92% Revenue, year over year 1.9× the revenue of the same period a year earlier.
87% Google Shopping impression share Across the commercially relevant Shopping category.

Measured against the equivalent period twelve months earlier. Growth came from volume, not from price. These are the business's results over that period — paid media was one part of the system that produced them.

Book a call What we did

What we did here.

We didn't just optimise media. We changed the conditions the media was working inside.

01 · Offer

Made the product easier to buy

It had been sold on one fixed upfront price — the first objection every visitor met. We introduced multiple payment options and pushed them forward through the journey, so the question changed from “do I want to spend this now?” to “which payment option works for me?” Not a discount. Less friction.

02 · Signals

Trained the algorithms on real customers

Optimisation had been running on shallow site behaviour. We fed conversion and customer data back to the platforms so they could learn what a valuable buyer actually looks like. Advertising algorithms are systems to be trained, not black boxes handed a budget.

03 · Meta

Created the demand

Meta was not asked to behave like search. Its job was to reach homes and workplaces before they were looking — one product range, several use cases — and to find which motivations pulled the right kind of person into the category.

04 · Google

Captured it

Once intent appears, selective presence wastes the demand created upstream. Shopping ran as the demand-capture engine for the whole category and reached roughly 87% impression share. Whenever someone entered the category looking for a solution, we wanted to be there.

05 · The loop between them

Creative learning

Every month, performance data told us which messages and use cases attracted buyers rather than browsers — and that answer built the next month's creative. Judged on what happened after the click, never on cheap clicks.

How we did it.

One loop, run every month. Each turn made the next one sharper, which is why it compounded rather than plateaued.

Feeds the next cycle

Performance marketing doesn't scale in isolation. Pricing decides how much traffic converts, conversion data decides what the algorithms learn, and what they learn decides which traffic arrives next. We changed them together rather than one at a time — and the result showed up as a business selling more than twice as many units year over year, not as a better-looking set of campaign metrics.
